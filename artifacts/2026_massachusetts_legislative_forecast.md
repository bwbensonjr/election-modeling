# 2026 Massachusetts Legislative Forecast

## Contested races ranked by competitiveness

The current 14-day forecast covers 57 contested Massachusetts legislative races in the November 3, 2026 election: 47 for State Representative and 10 for State Senate. The closest contest is 9th Norfolk State Representative, where Thomas R. Melville (D-Medfield) has a 44.8% Democratic win probability against incumbent Marcus S. Vaughn. Four races fall between 40% and 60%; the model favors Democrats in 43 contests and their opponents in 14.

Posterior draws average 42.0 Democratic wins across the listed contests, with a median of 42 and a 90% range of 39 to 45. These are wins among the 57 contested races, not total chamber seats.

Finance data were frozen at the 2026-10-20 cutoff. All target races had complete finance data, so each used the selected PVI, incumbency, and fundraising model. No target generated a support warning.

In 11 of the races the Democrat's only opponent reached the ballot by nomination papers rather than a party primary. Party letters are D (Democratic), R (Republican), U (Unenrolled), I (Independent), WP (Workers Party), and GWV (Gulf War Veteran). Where a Republican is on the ballot, the Republican is the comparison candidate, as in the model's training data; otherwise it is the sole non-Democrat. Opponent party is not a model predictor, so these races are forecast the same way as races against Republicans.

Competitiveness is measured by closeness to a 50% Democratic win probability. A positive model margin favors the Democrat. Intervals are the 90% posterior predictive range in percentage points.

## Change from earlier snapshots

This forecast revises the 46-race 14-day snapshot published on October 23. That snapshot built its race list from party primary nominees only, so it left out the 11 races against unenrolled, independent, and minor-party candidates. The model and its training data are unchanged. The Democrat is favored in all 11 added races; the closest is 2nd Worcester, where Michael Joseph Nicholson (D-Gardner) has an 80.8% win probability against unenrolled John D. Reilly, Jr. In the 46 races carried over, margins moved by at most 0.5 points and win probabilities by at most 2.3 points. The model's posterior is the same, but adding races changes how the random predictive draws fall to each race. That is also why 9th Norfolk, rather than 8th Bristol, is now the closest race. The 46-race snapshot averaged 31.3 Democratic wins and remains published unchanged as the record made on its date.

The 60-day snapshot, frozen at the 2026-09-04 finance cutoff, averaged 31.0 Democratic wins across the same 46 races; the original 14-day snapshot averaged 31.3. The average race margin moved 1.6 points. The largest moves were 10th Bristol (+43.8 to +24.7), 1st Bristol and Plymouth (+14.9 to +20.1), and 35th Middlesex (+55.8 to +60.0). The 10th Bristol move follows the opponent's receipts rising from zero to $3,950, which sharply narrows a log receipt ratio that began at an extreme. All snapshots remain published unchanged.

## Model and interpretation

The forecast uses a Bayesian Gaussian regression of Democratic margin on district PVI, incumbency, and the log ratio of Democratic to opponent receipts measured 14 days before the election, over the trailing 365 days. It was trained on eligible completed races through November 5, 2024. The response is the two-candidate margin against the Republican where one ran, and against the strongest non-Democrat otherwise.

Race intervals include coefficient uncertainty and residual race-level variation. Chamber draws preserve dependence represented by shared model parameters, but they do not add a shared 2026 statewide election shock.

Among the 47 listed House contests, the median is 33 Democratic wins with a 90% range of 30 to 36. Among the 10 listed Senate contests, the median is 9 with a 90% range of 8 to 10.
